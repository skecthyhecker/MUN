The Vision · दृष्टि

A Council Cast in the Colours of Bharat

Navdoot Parishad 1.0 is Air Force Golden Jubilee Institute’s premier Model United Nations — a gathering where the discipline of diplomacy meets the depth of Indian tradition. Delegates step into the roles of nations, negotiating crises, drafting resolutions, and finding, in argument, the art of listening.

नव — the new. दूत — the messenger. The Parishad is an assembly of envoys: first-timers and veterans, host school and visiting delegations, all under one gavel.

How allotment works

A live matrix of fifty nations. A verification engine. An automatic confirmation after ₹5 is received.

1. You rank three councils

UNGA, NCS, IP, UNDP, UNHRC, UNCSW, UNODC, IAEA, WTO. Preference 1 is tried first, then 2, then 3. If UNGA is among your choices, you also rank up to three countries from the live matrix.

2. The engine checks the seat

Available seats stay open. Seats already confirmed are Taken. A short Hold prevents two delegates from grabbing the same country while payment is in flight.

3. Payment seals the letter

Pay ₹5 to +91 84476 82098. When the transaction is recorded, the highest available preference is confirmed and a registration ID is issued. You will see a clear message: we have received your money.

Who may select which countries

Delegates from AFGJI, TAFS (The Air Force School), and AFBBS may choose any open seat in the matrix, including the first eight nations and Pakistan.

Individual delegates and other schools see those nine seats as blocked. Every other country in the fifty-nation list remains open until it is taken.

The public wording is simple: open, blocked, or taken. The engine does the rest.

Council briefs

Nine rooms, nine tempers

Each committee is a different kind of conversation. UNGA is the wide chamber. NCS is the locked room. IP is the corridor with a notebook. UNDP asks what growth is for. UNHRC asks who is protected. UNCSW writes equality into operative clauses. UNODC follows the money and the powder. IAEA weighs the atom. WTO bargains the tariff.

This edition’s live matrix is UNGA. Rank it first if you want a country now. Rank it second or third if you want the engine to try another council first and fall back to the Assembly.

First-timers are not a lesser category. The Parishad is built so a delegate with zero MUNs can still find a voice — with a primer, a patient Executive Board, and a fee that is only five rupees.

UNGA — United Nations General Assembly

Flagship council of Navdoot Parishad 1.0. Fifty-nation matrix. Three country preferences. Confirmation after payment.

The General Assembly is where every member state has a voice. Expect moderated caucus, unmoderated lobbying, draft resolutions, and a closing vote. Portfolios that are blocked on the public matrix remain available to AFGJI, TAFS, and AFBBS delegates until they are confirmed.

NCS — National Crisis Summit

Cabinet tempo. Directives instead of long drafts. Updates from the crisis staff.

A crisis committee rewards speed, coalition, and a calm head. Agendas and the exact cabinet will be released with the background guide. Rank NCS if you want pressure more than procedure.

IP — International Press

Reporters, photographers, editors. You do not vote. You decide what the conference remembers.

IP covers every council. Pieces, captions, and a conference paper. Rank IP if your craft is language or the lens — not the country placard.

UNDP · UNHRC · UNCSW

Development, rights, and the status of women — three chambers of the social pillar.

UNDP works in programmes and indicators. UNHRC works in testimony and law. UNCSW works in the gap between principle and practice. All three will open with dedicated agendas in the sessions ahead; ranking them now places you in the queue.

UNODC · IAEA · WTO

Crime, the atom, and trade — three chambers of the hard ledger.

UNODC follows trafficking routes. IAEA follows safeguards and the dual-use problem. WTO follows schedules, subsidies, and dispute. Technical, but never dry if the delegate has done the reading.

Indicative agendas

UNGA

Reform of the veto in matters of mass atrocity. Financing of peacekeeping. Climate-linked displacement as a question of international cooperation. Delegates will be asked to produce a draft resolution that can survive a divided floor.

NCS

A domestic crisis with foreign edges: energy, information, and a neighbour that will not sit still. Directives, press releases, and a cabinet that must agree before the next update lands.

IP

Cover the opening ceremony, one council in depth, and a feature on a first-time delegate. Photography assignments from the dais and the unmod circles. Editorial meeting each evening.

UNDP

South-South cooperation after a decade of stalled SDG markers. Digital public infrastructure. The politics of climate finance for middle-income states.

UNHRC

Protection of journalists in conflict. Civic space and the right to assemble. A country situation to be named in the background guide.

UNCSW

Economic participation after the pandemic generation. Education in emergencies. The gap between statute and street.

UNODC

Synthetic drugs and postal routes. Wildlife crime as organised crime. Recovery of proceeds across jurisdictions.

IAEA

Safeguards in a world of small modular reactors. Nuclear security of civilian material. Technical cooperation without leakage.

WTO

Food security and export bans. Digital trade and data localisation. Special and differential treatment — still a live fight.

What the engine does not do

It does not invent a seat that is taken. It does not show a blocked country as open to a delegate who is not from AFGJI, TAFS, or AFBBS. It does not confirm a portfolio before ₹5 is recorded. After payment you will see, in plain language, that the money has been received.

What you bring

A school ID. Formal western or Indian formal wear as set out in the handbook. A notepad. Curiosity. The rest — placard, badge, meals across the days — is in the fee.

From the Secretariat

A letter to the first-timer

If you have never spoken in a committee, you are not late. The Parishad was named for messengers, not for veterans. Read the guide. Write one sentence you believe. Raise your placard when that sentence is needed.

If you have done five MUNs, you are not owed the room. The same gavel falls for everyone. Rank honestly. Pay the five rupees. Show up on time.

Instagram and Discord are the public square between now and November. The handbook is the law of the floor. The matrix is the law of the seat.
